State-to-fate measurement for translational oncology

Preserve the history of the living tumor cell.

VitraCell is developing a low-input functional oncology platform designed to preserve the identity of original viable tumor cells through treatment and connect early response with eventual fate and molecular identity. Vimeran is the developing viable-cell recovery layer; Delta State Response (ΔSR) is the research framework for asking whether cell history adds useful information beyond baseline and endpoint measurements.

Preclinical research and development stage. Vimeran is under development and has not been clinically validated. Longitudinal same-cell response measurement remains a research hypothesis. Not intended for diagnosis or treatment selection.

The thesis in one minute

Drug response is dynamic. Most measurements are snapshots.

VitraCell is testing a specific question: when the identity of an original viable tumor cell is preserved through controlled perturbation, does its early response history add useful information beyond baseline and endpoint measurements alone?

The information gap

Most assays tell you what is present. We are asking what happened to the cell.

Population averages, destructive endpoints and post-treatment survivor analysis can be highly informative, but they can lose the history connecting an original living cell to its eventual fate. VitraCell is testing whether preserving that history adds information worth acting on in drug development.

01

Snapshots can lose the path

Baseline and endpoint measurements can show where a cell population started and where it ended without revealing the intermediate response of the exact cells that survived, recovered or died.

02

Expansion creates material, not history

Growing surviving cells can provide more material for study, but it cannot reconstruct the pretreatment state and early response of the original cell before culture and selection occurred.

03

The commercial test is decision value

VitraCell only creates value if identity-preserved history changes what a drug-development team does next: which condition, cell population or mechanism deserves deeper and more expensive investigation.

From cell state to cell fate

One cell. One identity. One continuous response record.

The proposed VitraCell data object links the state of an original viable cell before treatment to what that same cell does during perturbation, what ultimately happens to it, and—where feasible—its terminal molecular phenotype. The hypothesis is that this continuity may contain information that separate-cell or endpoint-only workflows cannot reconstruct.

ΔSR framework

Measure change relative to the same cell's baseline.

Delta State Response is the research framework for testing whether repeated, identity-linked observations contain incremental information beyond baseline plus endpoint measurements.

Evidence boundary

The biology remains to be proven.

Same-cell tracking, longitudinal imaging and state-to-fate research already exist. VitraCell's question is narrower: can a low-input workflow preserve useful state-to-fate information in original viable tumor cells where material, expansion or destructive analysis limits what can be learned?

Vimeran viable-cell acquisition layer

Recover the cell without making recovery the whole product.

Vimeran is being developed to recover viable tumor-associated cells from blood while preserving them for downstream measurement. Its strategic role is to make increasingly scarce original cells available to the same identity-preserved state-to-fate workflow—not to assume that rarity alone creates value.

The evidence program

Prove the information first. Add scarcity second.

VitraCell is separating the biological question from the engineering challenge. The first gate is whether same-cell history adds meaningful information in controlled models. The second is whether that advantage survives low-input, harder-to-replace material. Vimeran then becomes the acquisition layer for rare viable cells from blood.

Gate 1Controlled models

Prove that history adds information.

Start with abundant, well-characterized models and compare full same-cell history against simpler baseline and endpoint measurements using independent fate readouts.

  • Known treatment-sensitive and resistant biology
  • Prespecified longitudinal measurements
  • Matched vehicle and repeat-measure controls
  • Held-out experimental runs for validation
Gate 2Low-input translation

Show why the assay matters when material is limited.

Move only after the signal is real. Test whether the same approach remains informative in treatment-survivor, patient-derived or otherwise limited material where expansion or destructive sampling can obscure the original state.

  • Quantify starting-cell requirements
  • Measure identity retention and dropout
  • Compare information per starting cell
  • Determine whether downstream decisions change
Gate 3Rare-cell acquisition

Bring Vimeran into the validated assay.

Only after the information layer proves useful does rare-cell recovery become commercially powerful: Vimeran can extend the state-to-fate workflow toward viable cells recovered directly from blood.

  • Whole-blood recovery and background depletion
  • Post-recovery viability and repeatability
  • Same-cell re-identification
  • Expansion-free rare-cell feasibility
What is not being claimedVimeran and longitudinal same-cell measurement have not been clinically validated and do not currently predict drug response, improve treatment selection, or demonstrate superiority to ctDNA or established CTC platforms.

The science behind the hypothesis

Published biology supports the question. VitraCell still has to prove the answer.

Independent research supports several premises behind VitraCell's development program: viable CTCs can support functional study; intact cells provide information that cell-free analytes cannot preserve; and therapeutic perturbation can produce measurable electrical and mechanical state changes at the single-cell level. These findings establish scientific plausibility—not validation of Vimeran™ or ΔSR.

01

Viable CTCs enable functional study

Peer-reviewed work has described viable CTC enrichment, culture and functional investigation, including drug-screening applications. The scarcity of CTCs remains a major technical constraint.

Pantel & Alix-Panabières, Clinical Chemistry →
02

CTCs can complement ctDNA

Recent reviews distinguish viable CTCs from ctDNA because intact cells retain phenotypic and functional information, supporting their study as a complementary liquid-biopsy analyte.

Recent CTC precision-oncology review →
03

Drug exposure can change cell state

Single-cell impedance studies have detected heterogeneous drug-associated electrical responses, including signals observed before overt phenotypic change in experimental cancer-cell systems.

Chemical Science single-cell study →
04

The unresolved ΔSR question

Recent work continues to document drug-induced mechanical and electrical state transitions in tumor cells. What remains unproven is whether an early, reproducible trajectory in rare viable patient-derived tumor cells provides useful nonredundant information alongside established endpoints. That is the question ΔSR is intended to test.

2026 mechanophenotype study →

Evidence boundary: the cited studies were performed using their own platforms, models and experimental conditions. They do not validate Vimeran™, establish ΔSR performance, or demonstrate clinical utility.

Computational feasibility

Stress-test the hypothesis before spending wet-lab capital.

VitraCell has completed a synthetic computational feasibility study to formalize the ΔSR question and identify failure modes involving identity, noise, sampling and validation design. The study is a hypothesis stress test—not biological evidence and not assay performance.

Pharma, CRO and translational research partners

Bring us a response question, not a generic technology demo.

VitraCell is seeking teams with a defined treatment-response, persistence or resistance question where the history of an original living cell could plausibly change what gets tested next. The goal is a bounded study with prespecified controls, costs and a clear go-or-stop decision.

01

Define the decision

Start with the sponsor's actual development question, sample constraint and required evidence—not a generic technology demonstration.

02

Set the requirements

Agree on models, compounds, controls, exposure conditions, molecular markers and acceptance criteria before work begins.

03

Clear the technical gate

Establish repeatability and the relevant response signal in controlled material before advancing to scarce patient-derived samples.

04

Run a bounded pilot

Execute a defined feasibility study with a research report, transparent limitations and a clear go-or-stop decision.

Best initial fit

Teams with a biological response question worth testing in living cells.

The initial study model is deliberately narrow: define the biological question together, select an appropriate model and perturbation, establish early measurement time points and trusted downstream endpoints, then determine whether the longitudinal signal is worth pursuing further.

  • Pharma and biotech translational-oncology teams
  • Resistance, persistence and combination-development programs
  • Oncology CRO and biomarker-development groups
  • Patient-derived model and low-input assay teams

The company

Founder-led, evidence-driven and built in South Carolina.

VitraCell was founded to address an information problem in precision oncology: endpoint measurements can characterize what remains, but they may not preserve the history of the original living cell that led to that outcome.

The company is developing an identity-preserved state-to-fate assay framework and Vimeran as a viable-cell acquisition layer. The scientific thesis remains falsifiable: VitraCell must demonstrate that same-cell history provides incremental, decision-relevant information before claiming a functional advantage in scarce patient-derived cells.
