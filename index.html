The translational gap

ctDNA can answer important molecular questions. Living cells can support different ones.

Translational medicine and oncology biomarker teams increasingly use ctDNA and liquid biopsy to study tumor-derived molecular signals in blood. A complementary viable-CTC layer could support different research questions that require intact cells, including phenotype, heterogeneity, treatment-resistance biology and downstream cell-based characterization.

01

Different analytes, different information

ctDNA captures tumor-derived molecular material in plasma. Intact circulating tumor cells preserve whole-cell features that can support a different set of research measurements.

02

Living-cell questions require living cells

Questions involving cell morphology, biophysical phenotype, recovery or controlled ex vivo perturbation cannot be answered from cell-free DNA alone.

03

The opportunity is complementary

VitraCell's research hypothesis is that viable-cell data may add biological context when analyzed alongside ctDNA and other translational study endpoints.

Vimeran™ viable-cell platform

Recover the cell. Preserve its identity. Measure what changes.

The Vimeran™ Platform is being developed as a label-free viable CTC isolation, enrichment and characterization workflow for translational oncology research. The intended output is a recoverable population of viable rare cells with preserved identity and reproducible cell-level measurements that can be compared across study conditions and alongside ctDNA findings—not a replacement for ctDNA.

The evidence program

What happens before the cancer cell dies?

The near-term objective is to establish viable-cell recovery, background depletion, post-processing viability and reproducible characterization. A subsequent research objective is to test ΔSR (Delta-State Response): whether an early, reproducible change in viable-cell state following controlled therapeutic perturbation is associated with established downstream response endpoints.

Current focusCTC foundation

Establish the viable rare-cell engine

Build the repeatable isolation and characterization foundation required before functional testing of rare patient-derived cells is credible.

  • Label-free recovery of viable circulating tumor cells
  • Recovery, background-cell depletion and post-processing viability
  • Repeatable biophysical characterization
  • Traceable linkage to downstream cell identity
Next evidence stageΔSR research framework

Measure change, not just state

ΔSR (Delta-State Response) is VitraCell's research framework for testing whether a viable tumor cell or defined cell population undergoes an early, measurable state transition after controlled therapeutic perturbation—before conventional downstream viability or cell-death endpoints. It is a hypothesis to be tested—not a validated drug-response assay.

  • Establish a baseline viable-cell state
  • Apply a defined therapeutic perturbation under controlled conditions
  • Measure the resulting ΔState trajectory using reproducible cell-level features
  • Compare that trajectory with established molecular and cellular response endpoints
What is not being claimedThe Vimeran™ Platform and ΔSR have not been clinically validated and do not currently predict drug response, improve treatment selection, or demonstrate superiority to ctDNA or established CTC platforms.

The science behind the hypothesis

Published biology supports the question. VitraCell still has to prove the answer.

Independent research supports several premises behind VitraCell's development program: viable CTCs can support functional study; intact cells provide information that cell-free analytes cannot preserve; and therapeutic perturbation can produce measurable electrical and mechanical state changes at the single-cell level. These findings establish scientific plausibility—not validation of Vimeran™ or ΔSR.

01

Viable CTCs enable functional study

Peer-reviewed work has described viable CTC enrichment, culture and functional investigation, including drug-screening applications. The scarcity of CTCs remains a major technical constraint.

CTCs can complement ctDNA

Recent reviews distinguish viable CTCs from ctDNA because intact cells retain phenotypic and functional information, supporting their study as a complementary liquid-biopsy analyte.

03

Drug exposure can change cell state

Single-cell impedance studies have detected heterogeneous drug-associated electrical responses, including signals observed before overt phenotypic change in experimental cancer-cell systems.

04

The unresolved ΔSR question

Recent work continues to document drug-induced mechanical and electrical state transitions in tumor cells. What remains unproven is whether an early, reproducible trajectory in rare viable patient-derived tumor cells provides useful nonredundant information alongside established endpoints. That is the question ΔSR is intended to test.

Evidence boundary: the cited studies were performed using their own platforms, models and experimental conditions. They do not validate Vimeran™, establish ΔSR performance, or demonstrate clinical utility.

The company

Founder-led, evidence-driven and built in South Carolina.

VitraCell was founded by Gregory Moore to address a practical gap in precision oncology: ctDNA can provide powerful molecular information, but cell-free DNA does not preserve a living tumor cell for phenotypic study, recovery or downstream cell-based research.

The company is developing the Vimeran™ Platform for viable rare-cell recovery, identity preservation and characterization. Vimeran is the cell-acquisition and measurement foundation; ΔSR is the downstream research framework for asking how viable-cell state changes under controlled perturbation. The near-term hypothesis is that these viable-cell data may complement ctDNA in translational oncology studies. Every broader claim remains gated by evidence.
