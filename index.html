The translational gap

ctDNA can answer important molecular questions. Living cells can support different ones.

Translational teams increasingly use ctDNA to study tumor-derived molecular signals in blood. A complementary viable-cell layer could support separate research questions that require intact cells, including cell phenotype, heterogeneity and downstream characterization.

01

Different analytes, different information

ctDNA captures tumor-derived molecular material in plasma. Intact circulating tumor cells preserve whole-cell features that can support a different set of research measurements.

02

Living-cell questions require living cells

Questions involving cell morphology, biophysical phenotype, recovery or controlled ex vivo perturbation cannot be answered from cell-free DNA alone.

03

The opportunity is complementary

VitraCell's research hypothesis is that viable-cell data may add biological context when analyzed alongside ctDNA and other translational study endpoints.

The platform thesis

Recover the cell. Characterize it. Pair the data.

MIPS-5 is being developed as a label-free viable-CTC workflow for translational research. The intended output is a recoverable population of viable rare cells plus cell-level research measurements that can be compared with ctDNA findings from the same study—not a replacement for ctDNA.

The evidence program

One platform. Two disciplined evidence gates.

The near-term objective is to establish viable CTC recovery and characterization, then evaluate whether paired viable-cell measurements add useful research context to ctDNA-enabled translational studies. Functional-response capability remains a later development objective.

Current focusCTC foundation

Establish the viable rare-cell engine

Build the repeatable isolation and characterization foundation required before functional testing of rare patient-derived cells is credible.

  • Label-free recovery of viable circulating tumor cells
  • Recovery, background-cell depletion and post-processing viability
  • Repeatable biophysical characterization
  • Traceable linkage to downstream cell identity
Next evidence stagePaired translational data

Evaluate what viable-cell data adds beside ctDNA

In proposed translational studies, VitraCell will evaluate predefined research hypotheses by comparing viable-cell measurements with ctDNA and other study endpoints. This is an exploratory research question, not a claim of clinical utility.

  • Paired blood collection and analyte workflows where feasible
  • Comparison of ctDNA findings with viable-CTC recovery and cell-level features
  • Exploratory longitudinal concordance and discordance analysis
  • Predefined feasibility endpoints with transparent limitations
What is not being claimedMIPS-5 has not been clinically validated and does not currently predict drug response, improve treatment selection, or demonstrate superiority to ctDNA or established CTC platforms.

Translational study partners

Already generating ctDNA data? Add a viable-cell research question.

VitraCell is seeking ctDNA developers, pharmaceutical and biotechnology translational teams, and research partners interested in evaluating whether a viable-cell layer can add complementary biological context to an existing study.

01

Define the decision

Start with the sponsor's actual development question, sample constraint and required evidence—not a generic technology demonstration.

02

Set the requirements

Agree on models, compounds, controls, exposure conditions, molecular markers and acceptance criteria before work begins.

03

Clear the technical gate

Establish repeatability and the relevant response signal in controlled material before advancing to scarce patient-derived samples.

04

Run a bounded pilot

Execute a defined feasibility study with a research report, transparent limitations and a clear go-or-stop decision.

Teams already generating ctDNA data and asking what biology remains unmeasured.

The company

Founder-led, evidence-driven and built in South Carolina.

VitraCell was founded by Gregory Moore to address a practical gap in precision oncology: ctDNA can provide powerful molecular information, but cell-free DNA does not preserve a living tumor cell for phenotypic study, recovery or downstream cell-based research.

The company is developing MIPS-5 as a platform for viable rare-cell recovery and characterization. Its near-term research hypothesis is that viable-cell data may complement ctDNA in translational oncology studies; functional-response capability remains a later development objective. Every broader claim remains gated by evidence.
