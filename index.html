Viable rare-cell intelligence

Rare cells carry more than a signal. They carry a living story.

VitraCell is developing a label-free microfluidic workflow designed to recover viable circulating tumor cells and connect their live biophysical behavior with same-cell molecular characterization.

The unmet need

Genomic fragments can identify change. Living cells can reveal phenotype.

What established tools describe

  • Genomic alterations

The research question

Which viable tumor-cell subpopulation is persisting, and how is it changing over time?

That is the gap between measuring disease and building treatment-response intelligence.

The platform

Preserve the cell and its identity through the workflow.

The development architecture combines physical pre-enrichment with dielectrophoretic purification, followed by collection for molecular confirmation and future analytical integration.

01

Whole blood

Begin with a routine peripheral blood specimen and preserve the rare-cell population for staged processing.

02

Physical pre-enrichment

Reduce red blood cells and platelets while retaining larger nucleated cells for downstream purification.

03

DEP purification

Use intrinsic electrical behavior to refine the candidate rare-cell population without antibody capture.

04

Traceable cell record

Connect live biophysical measurement with downstream molecular confirmation at the same-cell level.

Current technical objectiveDemonstrate repeatable recovery, viability, background-cell depletion and traceable same-cell characterization.

The company

Founder-led and built for disciplined translation.

VitraCell was founded by Gregory Moore to close a practical gap in precision oncology: molecular information can help identify a target, but it does not always describe how a living tumor-cell population is behaving now.

The company is headquartered in Lexington, South Carolina and is building its development program through scientific and clinical collaboration.
