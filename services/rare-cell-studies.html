What the sponsored study includes

  1. Define the decision: what would your team do differently if the study succeeds?
  2. Define the cell population: tumor cell, therapeutic immune cell or another defined living-cell model.
  3. Define the perturbation: drug exposure, target-cell encounter, repeated stimulation, culture variable or other controlled challenge.
  4. Keep the comparator: establish the existing endpoint or assay that the longitudinal layer must complement.
  5. Define the fate endpoint: survival, death, recovery, persistence, exhaustion, cytotoxic activity, expansion or another independently measured outcome.
  6. Define the decision rule: agree in advance what result supports expansion, refinement or closure of the study question.

What you receive

  • Sponsor-specific study architecture and statement-of-work scope.
  • Prespecified controls, acceptance criteria and analysis plan.
  • Identity-linked longitudinal dataset integrated with the selected fate or molecular endpoint.
  • Comparison of full history with simpler baseline and endpoint approaches.
  • Sponsor-facing decision report with reproducibility assessment, limitations and go / refine / stop recommendation.

How VitraCell intends to execute capital-efficiently

VitraCell acts as the commercial and study-integration layer. Experimental execution can use the sponsor's laboratory, qualified academic/shared infrastructure or specialized external providers depending on the model, biosafety requirements, assay and timeline. VitraCell does not need to duplicate every laboratory capability internally before the commercial question is proven.

Research-use service

Each engagement is defined by a statement of work covering the cell model, measurement modality, execution site, controls, endpoints, deliverables, timeline and cost. Clinical treatment-selection, lot-release and diagnostic claims are outside the present service scope.

What we need from the sponsor

  • A real asset, mechanism, cell product or response question.
  • The current model or sample context.
  • The benchmark endpoint or assay already trusted by the team.
  • The decision that the result must inform.
